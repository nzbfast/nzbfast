Usenet client download benchmarks

The raw numbers from a set of client download rounds, without commentary. Each table races one current build of each client on identical hardware, the same five providers, TLS everywhere, runs interleaved, and every leg's output checked byte for byte. Rows are listed in a fixed order in every table, not ranked by result. Nothing on this page is highlighted.

Method

How the numbers were taken

  • Builds: NZBGet 26.3-testing, SABnzbd 5.1.1, nzbfast 1.2.2, Weaver 0.7.8 (its newest shipped binary, identity checked by hash), rustnzb 1.4.5. One build of each client across every table on this page.
  • Configuration: each client at its documented best settings. SABnzbd ran pipelining_requests=8; NZBGet ran ArticleCache, DirectWrite, DirectUnpack and ParQuick; rustnzb ran its documented config. nzbfast ran as shipped: on the 1 Gbit and slower rounds its connection governor held 25 connections; on the 10 GbE round it ran 50 connections (10 per server). The other clients ran their configured connection counts, in the hundreds.
  • End state: the clock stops at a verified, extracted, usable file, not at "download finished".
  • Measurement: elapsed time, process-tree peak RSS and CPU time sampled at 2 Hz, device I/O in GiB, wire bytes in decimal GB. Three repetitions per client per fixture with the order rotated; the figure quoted is the median unless a footnote says otherwise. Every leg's output was compared byte for byte against a reference checksum.
  • Hardware: the 250 Mbit, 500 Mbit and 1 Gbit rounds ran on a 20-core Apple Silicon machine (the two slower rates shaped from the 1 Gbit line, the shaped rate verified by an independent probe: 248 and 496 Mbit). The 10 GbE round ran on an Apple Silicon machine on a 10 GbE line. The Windows round ran on a native Windows 10 GbE machine with a TLC system drive sustaining 0.99 GB/s of writes.
  • Dates: the 1 Gbit round ran 23 August 2026; the 250 Mbit, 500 Mbit, 10 GbE, Windows and damaged-post rounds ran 24 August 2026.
