# Environment variables

Every `NZBFAST_*` variable read by the code in `crates/*/src`, grouped
Supported first. "Supported" means an operator may reasonably set it;
"Debug-only" covers rollout kill-switches, bench/tuning knobs, and test
harness plumbing - they can vanish or change meaning between releases.

Strings like `__NZBFAST_INDEX__`, `__NZBFAST_INDEXERS__`,
`__NZBFAST_SPOTS__`, `__NZBFAST_LOCALE__`, and `__NZBFAST_UI_TOKENS__`
also appear in the source but are HTML placeholder tokens substituted
into the embedded dashboard pages, not environment variables.

| `NZBFAST_STEER_DEPTH` | `1` arms M7b.2 depth steering: a server whose windowed (~10 s half-life) per-connection rate falls under 1/4 of the best other live server's tops its pipelines up to depth 1 instead of the configured window, restoring above 1/2 (hysteresis). Composes with stream mode (min of the two caps); a lone server, or a fleet with untrained rates, is never clamped | off | Experimental |
| `NZBFAST_TAIL_TAPER` | `1` arms the TODO 208 item 3 endgame depth taper: as the work still to be handed out falls toward one article per connection, connections top their pipelines up shallower, so the fleet reaches queue-dry holding about one article each instead of `window` each. The stretch between queue-dry and the last article is that in-flight set emptying - `connections x window` articles, 1.13-1.62 GB on the bench fleet whatever the job or the line - and roughly a fifth of it is line-idle, because an article parked behind a slow session cannot be handed to a fast connection that has gone idle. Tapering leaves that work in the QUEUE where it can still be rebalanced. Inert until the queue falls under `window x connections`, so a fast link, where deep pipelining is what earns the throughput, is untouched. When armed, the `[pool]` summary line carries `taper armed, min depth N` (or `taper armed, never bit`) so a run can show the knob arrived without inferring it from the tail it is measuring | off | Experimental |
| `NZBFAST_LINE_CAP` | TODO 208 item 1, the fleet cap: the whole fleet's connection budget, split equally across the servers and still under each account's own `connections` (a pinned server keeps its number). Measured on a 99 Mbit line with five providers: fleet 50 beats the configured 360 by 6.6% wall with zero stall-deadline kills, 13x less duplicate spend and 4.7x less RSS - and the floor rounds then found no rung below that where wall starts to rise, at 99 Mbit or at 247, where fleet 25 beat fleet 125 on wall, memory, cpu, duplicate wire, drain and latency alike. The best fleet does not scale with the line up to a gigabit, where fleet 20 finished a second ahead of fleet 360 - so `25` (5 connections on each of five providers) is the FLOOR, and the default is a curve that never goes below it (TODO 277). Above the floor it climbs in rungs of 5 sockets, planning 150 Mbit of line per socket, to a ceiling of `50`: a line has to read above 3.75 Gbit before the fleet grows at all, and reaches the ceiling at 7.5 Gbit. The ceiling is the measured knee and not an account maximum - on an 87 GB job at 10 GbE, wall is flat at 69-71 s for every fleet from 50 to 360 while cpu rises 2.3x across that flat, and the shipped 25 walled 90 s. The ceiling is also a fleet the SLOW-line ladder cleared - at 99 Mbit fleet 50 / 35 / 25 all walled 544-547 s - which is the bound on what a wrong reading can cost, since the anchor falls back to a typed line speed when nothing has been measured. The reading it sizes from is the daemon's persisted link anchor, so a fast install is on the knee from its SECOND job on; a run with no anchor at all (a CLI `get`, a prefetch sidecar, a daemon that has not finished a job) reads 0, and 0 is the floor. Within a run a governor may grow the fleet as the evidence improves, with hysteresis - a bigger reading has to hold three consecutive seconds, the rungs absorb anything smaller, and the fleet never falls - though it can only ever wake slots that were spawned, so today it binds where the spawn ceiling is above the live target. **The unit changed with the rule on 23 Aug 2026** - it used to be connections per Mbit, default `0.5` - so a stale `0.5` no longer parses and reads as OFF. Two halves: the daemon and the CLI both seed each job's fleet at the cap (no link anchor needed, unlike the per-Mbit rule this replaced, so a bench leg that wants a bigger fleet must now turn the cap off), and within a run the pool walks the live targets down to it in one step - that SHED half still stands down without the daemon's persisted `linkpeak` anchor, so a run with no independent estimate of its line is never resized DOWN mid-flight (the governor's raise is not behind that gate: it multiplies where the shed divides, so a reading that is too low can only leave the fleet where the seed put it). The §112 `live_tune` walker walks inside the cap. `0` (or any non-number) disables both halves, which is the bench A/B arm; any other number is a FIXED fleet for the whole run, governor included - a leg that typed a fleet size is asking for that fleet and not for a floor. The `[pool]` line carries `line cap N (K sheds)` when the in-run half ran, with ` raised from S` appended after the bracket when the governor grew the fleet | rate-aware, 25 to 50 | Supported |
| `NZBFAST_STALL_SHARE_AWARE` | `0` pins the flat 8 s no-progress stall deadline in place of the TODO 208 item 2 share-aware bound, `clamp(2 x mean body / (line peak / sharers), 8 s, 60 s)`, which stops a connection merely starved by a 360-way share being killed as stalled (311 false kills per job at 100 Mbit). The bound only stretches once the line gauge has trained, and the sharer count falls with the queue at the tail, so the last articles get the 8 s floor back | on | Supported |
| `NZBFAST_STALL_LIVE` | `0` pins the share-aware bound above to its 21 Aug shape for an A/B leg: sampled once at the start of each body read, and the flat 8 s floor until the run's own line peak has trained. On (default, TODO 208 item 2's warm-up fix) the bound is re-read once a second DURING a silence against the best line evidence the pool has - the run's trained peak, else the daemon's persisted link anchor (the figure the `NZBFAST_LINE_CAP` seed was sized from until that cap became a constant, and still the thing that arms its in-run shed; stamped per job from the second job on), else the gauge's own provisional reading a quarter time constant in. Why: the peak trains 7 s after the FIRST delivered body, which a 100 Mbit line under 360 connections lands 10-27 s into the run, so the whole first wave of bodies - read during the fleet's slow-start storm, the most starved moment of the run - was judged by the floor, and evidence the gauge learned while a connection was already quiet could never reach a wait armed before it. With the share-aware bound alone the 250 Mbit legs still logged 27-42 stall-deadline kills each; this is the half it could not reach | on | Supported |
| `NZBFAST_PEAK_ARRIVALS` | `0` pins the line gauge behind the two knobs above (the §202 saturation gate's `line peak`, which the share-aware stall bound divides) to its 21 Aug feed for an A/B leg: one fold per DELIVERED body. On (default, TODO 208 item 2's over-read fix) the gauge is fed per chunk as the bytes come off the socket. Why: a body is credited when it completes, but its bytes crossed the line over the whole transfer before that, and the gauge's age starts at its first fold - so every body in flight at that instant (on a fleet dialled together, every connection's first body) landed in one clump credited to a window that had barely opened. The trained peak then over-read the line by 10-35% on every banked shaped leg and by 70% on the fault rig (680 KB/s for a 400 KB/s line), and because the peak is a run max that never came back down: every stall bound of the run short by that share, and the gate reading less saturated than the line was (the `NZBFAST_LINE_CAP` fleet was sized off this reading too, until it became a constant). ## Debug-only

with any value.
